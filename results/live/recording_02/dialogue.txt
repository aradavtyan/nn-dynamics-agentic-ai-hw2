## Reading the result

**What is solid.** The optimization did something real and reproducible: validated initial enhancement 1.11593 ± 0.00388 (n=10) vs. validated final 1.50486 ± 0.00592 (n=10). That separation is ~60σ on the reported stds, so the pattern change is not a noise realization.

**What is not.** The gain is almost entirely a *background* effect, not a *focus* effect:

| quantity | value | reading |
|---|---|---|
| enhancement_gain | 1.34852 | local contrast F/B improved 35% |
| focus_intensity_gain | 0.852717 | focus got **dimmer** by ~15% |
| background_suppression_factor | 1.58144 | background got dimmer by ~37% |
| log-gain share (focus / background) | −0.533 / +1.533 | 153% of the log-contrast gain came from darkening B; focus dimming *subtracted* 53% |

So the loop found a DMD pattern that *subtracts light from the background strips* faster than it subtracts light from the focus. I will not call this "focusing" in any useful sense — a target that is dimmer in absolute terms is a dark spot, not a bright one — and I make no claim that the focus is the brightest point of the frame.

**Why this happened (diagnosis).** With g=4 there are only 16 binary macropixels, and the focus is 6×6 = 36 camera pixels. If the speckle grain is on the order of one camera pixel, the focus spans ~36 independent output modes while we have only 16 controllable inputs — a *deficit*. In that regime you cannot constructively build up the whole focus region, so the only remaining way to raise F/B is to lower B, which is exactly what the optimizer did. The initial enhancement already being 1.116 at a random pattern is consistent with a geometric/statistical bias (a centered 6×6 region being slightly brighter per pixel than the flanking strips), not with any prior focusing.

## Is a second experiment worthwhile?

Yes, and I'd spend it on *raising absolute focus intensity*, not on a repeat. Reasons: (1) budget allows it (3436 remaining, cap 2000/call); (2) the current artifact is a dark-focus solution, which is the wrong outcome for essentially any delivery application; (3) the diagnosed cause is a specific, testable DOF-vs-focus-size deficit, and a follow-up can attack it directly.

## Levers and trade-offs

- **Grouping factor g=2 → 64 macropixels.** Roughly quadruples the controllable degrees of freedom, which is what lets the loop build up a focus region at all. Cost: more iterations to converge and a weaker per-macropixel signal, so noise control matters more. g=1 (256) would give more DOF still, but with a 2000-acquisition cap it would be under-iterated and I'd rather keep budget for a third experiment.
- **Shrink the focus to 4×4 (16 px).** This directly improves the DOF-per-mode ratio (≈64 controls for ~16 modes instead of 16 for ~36) and concentrates energy into fewer modes. Risk: a smaller focus makes the F/B metric noisier and makes the initial enhancement more sensitive to geometry, so I only shrink moderately (4×4, not 2×2) and compensate with averaging.
- **Keep gap=2 / width=4 background.** Holding the background definition fixed keeps the metric comparable to call 01, so the change in focus_intensity_gain is interpretable.
